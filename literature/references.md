Below is a stricter literature list limited to **scholarly journal articles / peer-reviewed articles** that should be findable through **Google Scholar** and, for most of them, **Scopus/Web of Science**. I exclude policy reports, government pages, news, blogs, and working papers unless there is a journal version.

## A. Closest modern peer-reviewed articles, 2020+

### 1. Chen, M. (2023). “Best and brightest? The impact of student visa restrictiveness on international student migration.”

This is the closest match to your article. It uses U.S. F-1 visa refusal rates and studies how visa restrictiveness affects international student enrolment. The published version is in *Journal of Public Economics*. ([ScienceDirect][1])

Use it for: **student visa refusal rates, visa restrictiveness, international student migration, policy filtering**.

### 2. Gonnot, J. (2025). “Visa policy and international student migration: evidence from Canadian community colleges.”

Published in *Journal of Economic Geography*. It studies a Canadian visa-policy reform that introduced a stream with shorter processing and higher approval rates for qualifying student visa applicants. ([OUP Academic][2])

Use it for: **visa policy as a mechanism shaping student flows; financial/language requirements; approval-rate policy effects**.

### 3. Park, C. et al. (2022). “The impact of changing nonimmigrant visa policies on international students.”

Peer-reviewed and available in PMC. It focuses on how U.S. visa-policy changes affected international students, especially in the COVID-era policy context. ([PMC][3])

Use it for: **visa uncertainty, student migration disruption, policy sensitivity**.

### 4. Oduwaye, O. et al. (2023). “A Trend Analysis of the Challenges of International Students.”

Published in *SAGE Open*. This is broader than visa approval rates but useful for international-student barriers and challenges in host countries. ([Sage Journals][4])

Use it for: **student-mobility barriers and international-student experience**, but not as your core visa-statistics reference.

### 5. Gülel, D. (2025). “Navigating visa inequities: mobility as privilege in academia.”

Published in *Global Social Challenges Journal*. It is not student-visa-approval modelling, but directly useful for the theoretical framing of visa inequality and academic mobility privilege. ([bristoluniversitypressdigital.com][5])

Use it for: **visa inequity, academic mobility, Global South mobility barriers**.

### 6. Deutschmann, E. (2025). “A time penalty for the Global South? Inequalities in visa appointment wait times.”

This is highly relevant if you want to frame administrative friction as part of unequal mobility regimes. It is about visa appointment wait times rather than student approvals, but conceptually close. ([Wiley Online Library][6])

Use it for: **administrative inequality, Global South disadvantages, visa processing burden**.

### 7. Byrne, W. H. (2025). “Redefining the mobility paradigm in international law.”

Published in *International Journal of Law in Context*. Not student-specific, but useful for the legal-theoretical framing of mobility governance and visa regimes. ([Cambridge University Press & Assessment][7])

Use it for: **legal mobility regimes, structural constraints on movement**.

## B. Foundational peer-reviewed articles, all time

### 8. King, R., & Raghuram, P. (2013). “International Student Migration: Mapping the Field and New Research Agendas.”

Published in *Population, Space and Place*. This is one of the central articles for treating international students as migrants, not only as education consumers. ([Wiley Online Library][8])

Use it for: **positioning your topic inside international student migration studies**.

### 9. Mau, S., Gülzau, F., Laube, L., & Zaun, N. (2015). “The Global Mobility Divide: How Visa Policies Have Evolved over Time.”

Published in *Journal of Ethnic and Migration Studies*. Very important for visa inequality and nationality-based mobility stratification. ([Taylor & Francis Online][9])

Use it for: **global visa-policy inequality and unequal access to mobility**.

### 10. Neumayer, E. (2006). “Unequal access to foreign spaces: how states use visa restrictions to regulate mobility in a globalized world.”

Published in *Transactions of the Institute of British Geographers*. Foundational article on how states use visa restrictions and how this produces unequal access to foreign spaces. ([RGS-IBG Library][10])

Use it for: **country-level visa barriers and global inequality**.

### 11. Shamir, R. (2005). “Without Borders? Notes on Globalization as a Mobility Regime.”

Published in *Sociological Theory*. Classic theoretical paper arguing that globalization includes systems of closure, containment, risk management, and suspicion. ([Sage Journals][11])

Use it for: **mobility regime theory and the logic of suspicion/risk governance**.

### 12. Czaika, M., de Haas, H., & Villares-Varela, M. (2018). “The Global Evolution of Travel Visa Regimes.”

Peer-reviewed and available in PMC. It builds on the global visa-regime literature and is useful for broader historical context. ([PMC][12])

Use it for: **evolution of visa regimes, global mobility structures**.

### 13. Czaika, M., de Haas, H., & Villares-Varela, M. (2017). “Visa restrictions and economic globalisation.”

Published in *Applied Geography*. Useful for linking visa restrictions to economic globalization and international inequality. ([ScienceDirect][13])

Use it for: **economic globalization, visa restrictions, inequality of movement**.

### 14. Jena, F. (2013). “The determinants of United Kingdom student visa demand.”

Published in an economics journal. Older than your requested modern period, but directly relevant because it uses student visa demand and refusal-rate logic in the UK context. ([Springer][14])

Use it for: **student visa demand modelling and policy determinants**.

## C. How to use these in your literature review

For your paper, I would not present the literature as “visa approval rates in New Zealand,” because that exact niche is thin. I would write it like this:

1. **International student mobility is a migration field**, not only an education-market field.
   Use King & Raghuram.

2. **Visa systems create unequal access to mobility by nationality and country position.**
   Use Neumayer, Shamir, Mau et al., Czaika et al.

3. **Student visa policy changes and refusal/approval conditions shape student flows.**
   Use Chen, Gonnot, Park, Jena.

4. **Recent work increasingly frames visas as administrative and inequality-producing infrastructure.**
   Use Deutschmann, Gülel, Byrne.

## D. Best “core 8” for your article

If you want a tight literature review, I would prioritize these:

1. Chen 2023 — student visa refusal/restrictiveness.
2. Gonnot 2025 — visa policy and student migration.
3. King & Raghuram 2013 — international student migration field.
4. Neumayer 2006 — unequal access through visa restrictions.
5. Shamir 2005 — mobility regime theory.
6. Mau et al. 2015 — global mobility divide.
7. Czaika et al. 2018 — evolution of visa regimes.
8. Deutschmann 2025 — recent administrative visa inequality.

These are enough to build a respectable scholarly frame around your empirical INZ analysis.
